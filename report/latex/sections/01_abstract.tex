\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Most chess engines rely on explicit search trees, handcrafted heuristics, or spatial board tensors. This report introduces \textbf{Nebium}, a decoder-only causal transformer that learns chess strategy purely from coordinate move sequences in Universal Chess Interface (UCI) notation. Nebium combines Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) for relative ply distance encoding, SwiGLU gating, and RMSNorm pre-normalization. The architecture is trained on 245,293 tournament games from the Lichess Open Database using a 2,056-token Byte-Pair Encoding vocabulary.

Across scaling tiers, Nebium-Small (117M) achieves a validation cross-entropy loss of 2.727 nats (perplexity 15.30) and 100\% opening legality, while Nebium-Medium (345M) reaches 2.376 nats (perplexity 10.76), 36.4\% Top-1 move prediction accuracy, and 70.5\% Top-5 accuracy over 20 epochs. Nebium-Large (762M), trained to epoch 3 under dual-T4 compute limits, attains 80.0\% move legality and 6.071 nats validation loss, tracking early scaling trajectories in compute-bounded regimes. Dynamic legal move masking eliminates illegal move generation during deployment by filtering logits against board-state rules at inference time. These results demonstrate that autoregressive transformers acquire tactical rules and opening theory directly from 1D coordinate strings without spatial board representations, though unconstrained rollouts still exhibit coordinate drift in late game horizons.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Causal Transformers, Sequence Modeling, Rotary Position Embeddings, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, Chess Representation Learning.
\end{abstract}
