\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Most chess engines rely on explicit search trees, handcrafted heuristics, or spatial board tensors. This report introduces \textbf{Nebium}, a decoder-only causal transformer that learns chess strategy purely from coordinate move sequences in Universal Chess Interface (UCI) notation. Nebium combines Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) for relative ply distance encoding, SwiGLU gating, and RMSNorm pre-normalization. The architecture is trained on 245,293 tournament games from the Lichess Open Database using a 5,000-token Byte-Pair Encoding vocabulary.

On held-out validation games, Nebium achieves a cross-entropy loss of 1.88 (perplexity 6.55) and generates 96.8\% legal moves across the opening without search lookahead. With dynamic legal move masking, the model plays at an estimated 1845 Elo against Stockfish Depth 10. A 10-gram sequence audit confirms 0.00\% leakage between training and test sets. These results demonstrate that autoregressive transformers can acquire tactical rules and opening theory from 1D coordinate strings, though unconstrained rollouts still suffer spatial drift in late endgames.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Causal Transformers, Sequence Modeling, Rotary Position Embeddings, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, Chess Representation Learning.
\end{abstract}
