\subsection{Nebium Model Family and Parameter Scaling}
\label{sec:model_family}

To evaluate how model capacity affects move prediction and legality, Nebium was instantiated at three scales: Small (117M), Medium (345M), and Large (762M). All three variants share the core components defined in Section~\ref{sec:proposed_method} which are RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, and the 2,056-token BPE tokenizer. Scaling varies layer count ($L$), model dimension ($d_{\text{model}}$), and attention heads ($H$), while holding the per-head dimension at $d_k = 64$ to align with GPU Tensor Core memory tiles. Table~\ref{tab:family_overview} summarizes the tier specifications.

All linear projections omit bias parameters to avoid unregularized weights. While Small and Medium train through 20 epochs within 16~GB VRAM on dual NVIDIA T4s, the 762M tier required memory-saving adjustments: 8-bit AdamW reduced optimizer states from 8 to 2 bytes per parameter (saving over 4.5~GB VRAM) and gradient checkpointing recomputed intermediate activations. Large trained on a single T4 at micro-batch size 1 with 128 accumulation steps, holding peak memory at 14.5~GB.

Due to hardware session limits, Nebium-Large completed 3 epochs (12.0M tokens), reaching 80.0\% move legality and 6.071 nats validation loss, which matches the early learning rate of Nebium-Small at identical depth. The trained FP16 checkpoint and GGUF binary are published at \href{https://huggingface.co/nabin2004/nebium-large}{\texttt{nabin2004/nebium-large}} and \href{https://huggingface.co/nabin2004/nebium-large-gguf}{\texttt{nabin2004/nebium-large-gguf}}. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_model_configs} lists complete hyperparameter tables for all three configurations.
